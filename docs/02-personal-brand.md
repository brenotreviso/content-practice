# 2 · Personal brand

> **In one sentence:** a personal brand, here, is a short, honest description of what you repeatedly offer — not a costume and not a company.

## New words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Brand** | The pattern people start to recognise: what you talk about, how you sound, and how you look. |
| **Promise** | The one thing a viewer can expect from you. |
| **Voice** | How you sound: word choice, pace, humour, what you refuse to pretend. |
| **Boundaries** | What you will not film, say, or share. |
| **Brand sheet** | A one-page worksheet that holds the above. Template: [templates/brand-sheet.md](templates/brand-sheet.md). |

If the word “brand” makes you feel like you are being asked to become an influencer, replace it with “how I will show up”. Same job.

## The one-page brand sheet

Fill these lines. Keep each one short enough to read aloud in a breath.

1. **Who it is for.** One person, like Maya’s “someone who gets home late and still wants a real meal”.
2. **Who it is not for.** This is a kindness. Maya’s is not for restaurant reviews, meal-prep empires, or people who want a 20-step aesthetic kitchen.
3. **Promise.** “I show the small, useful version of ordinary life in a rented flat — cooking, mending, walking — without pretending I have it all sorted.”
4. **Voice.** Three adjectives and one “never”. Maya: warm, plain, slightly wry; never glossy, never a lecture.
5. **Look.** What will be on screen often enough that it feels like you: a phone, a kitchen counter, daylight, captions in a readable font. You can change this later. You do not need a logo this week.
6. **Boundaries.** Maya’s: no filming flatmates without a clear yes; no strangers’ faces on canal walks; no posting something she would be ashamed to have a colleague see; no pretending to be a professional cook.
7. **The name people will see.** Your real name, a first name, or a simple project name. Maya posts as herself. A separate persona is allowed; it is not required, and it will not hide you from everyone you know.

## You can be recognised without being a performer

Official platform advice is usually about being understandable, not about becoming a celebrity. YouTube’s [thumbnail and title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en) distinguish content for people who already know you from content for people who do not. Instagram’s ranking explainer talks about activity, relationships and predictions, not about having a “personal brand kit”.

So: consistency helps people recognise you. Perfection does not. A recognisable cover style, a way of starting a sentence, or a kitchen already in daylight is enough to begin.

## Maya

She writes the sheet above and sticks on one extra boundary: she will not wait until she likes her face on camera before she posts. Her first videos can be her hands and her voice. That is still her brand. The [brand sheet template](templates/brand-sheet.md) in this repo is already filled in with her answers, then left blank for you.

## Exercise

Copy [templates/brand-sheet.md](templates/brand-sheet.md) and fill it in for yourself in one sitting, 20 minutes maximum. If a line takes more than three drafts, write the rougher one. You can edit it after five posts.

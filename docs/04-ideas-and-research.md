# 4 · Ideas and research

> **In one sentence:** start from one thing you already care about, split it into many video ideas (a content tree), then use search and comments as clues — not as orders — for what people are asking.

## New words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Content tree** (or idea tree) | One “trunk” topic with “branches” (angles, questions, mistakes, myths). Worksheet: [templates/idea-tree.md](templates/idea-tree.md). |
| **Topic cluster** | A related idea from website SEO: one broad pillar, many focused pieces around it. |
| **Autocomplete** (Google and YouTube often say **predictions**) | The phrases that appear while you type in a search box. |
| **People Also Ask (PAA)** | A box of related questions Google may show after you search. |
| **Query** | The words someone types into search. |

## From one idea to many: the content tree

Take one pillar, not your whole life.

1. Write the **trunk** in the middle of the page (Maya: “weeknight cooking in a small kitchen”).
2. Add **branches**. Useful kinds of branch:
   - Questions (“what can I cook in 15 minutes?”)
   - Mistakes (“I always burn the onions”)
   - Myths (“you need a recipe with 14 ingredients”)
   - Before / after, or first time / tenth time
   - A day-in-the-life take on the same topic
   - A tool or ingredient you actually use
3. Each branch should be able to become **one video with one promise**. If a branch is still huge, split it again.

This is an adaptation of the **topic cluster** model [HubSpot described in 2017](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/topic-clusters-seo) for websites (a broad pillar page, plus focused pages that link back). We are using the shape for video ideas, not as a search-engine tactic, and not as something invented for Reels.

## How to use search — carefully

Search tells you how people *word* a need. It does not tell you that you must make that video, or that high demand will make a video travel. Stay inside your voice and pillars.

### Google Autocomplete and People Also Ask

Google’s Help page [How Google autocomplete predictions work](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/7368877?hl=en) says Autocomplete is a feature that generates **predictions** to help people finish a search they started. Predictions can be influenced by the language of the query, location, trending interest, and (if you are signed in) past searches. Google also publishes [How Google autocomplete works in Search](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/how-google-autocomplete-works-search/), which is careful to call them predictions, not suggestions, and to say Autocomplete is **not** the same as [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/).

**How to use it:** type your trunk, then type it again with “how”, “why”, “can I”, “without”, “for”. Write down phrases that appear. Do this in an unsigned window if you do not want your own history to dominate.

After you press enter, you may see **People Also Ask**. Google’s Help page [How Google’s featured snippets work](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/9351707?hl=en) mentions a “People also ask” section as one place featured snippets can appear. Treat those questions as candidate branches. **Unverified:** we did not rely on unofficial descriptions of how PAA questions are chosen; Google has not, in the pages we opened, published a full public algorithm for that box.

### YouTube search predictions and Studio research

YouTube Help, [Find videos faster](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9872296?hl=en), explains **search predictions**: they come from the terms you enter, other people’s searches (including trending searches), and, if you are signed in and settings allow, your own history. Predictions “aren’t the answer to your search” and “aren’t statements by other people or YouTube”.

YouTube Studio also has tools for exploring what people search. Help: [Explore trends on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/11962757?hl=en). As of a July 2026 note on that page, Studio has been rolling out an updated experience, and some creators see a **Research** area rather than (or as well as) a Trends tab. If you do not see it yet, use search predictions on YouTube itself, plus comments.

YouTube also explains [how search ranking works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438?hl=en) in terms of relevance, engagement and quality — useful later when you write titles, not for choosing a personality.

### TikTok search suggestions and “Others searched for”

TikTok shows related search prompts in and around search results. The label **“Others searched for”** has been described in independent research ([tiktok-audit, 2024](https://tiktok-audit.com/blog/2024/Search-Suggestions/)) and in UK Parliament oral evidence, where TikTok’s Ali Law said search suggestions are “generally auto-generated on the basis of what people are searching for” ([oral evidence transcript](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/15413/pdf/)).

**Unverified as official documentation:** when we checked on 20 Nov 2026, we did not find a TikTok Help Centre page that uses the exact label “Others searched for”. Use the feature if you can see it in the app, treat it as auto-generated related queries, and re-check the [platform snapshot](platform-snapshot.md) if the label changes. Also remember that suggested searches can be wrong or harmful; they are not a brief.

### AnswerThePublic-style tools

[AnswerThePublic](https://answerthepublic.com/) is a commercial tool that visualises questions and phrases around a topic. Its own [starter guide](https://answerthepublic.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/13932754469275-AnswerThePublic-Starter-Guide) (updated 21 October 2026) says it pulls autocomplete data from several platforms, including Google, Bing, Amazon, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, and also shows Google People Also Ask.

**Live check, 20 Nov 2026:** the site loaded. Free versus paid limits change; check the site yourself before you rely on a particular report. Similar tools exist; only use one if it still exists when you look, and do not treat its volumes as official Google or TikTok numbers.

### Comments

Comments on videos like yours are a messy, useful mine: they contain real wording (“wait, do you have to soak the beans?”). They are not a vote. One loud comment is not a trend. Never harvest or publish someone’s private details.

## From a question to a video

Use this four-part rule:

1. **One question** (a branch on the tree).
2. **One promise** (the first line of the script).
3. **One format** (chapter 5).
4. **One payoff** (the answer, honestly scoped).

If you cannot say the promise in one breath, the idea is still too big.

## Maya’s tree (teaching example)

Trunk: *weeknight cooking in a small kitchen.*

Branches she actually writes down after a Google and YouTube pass (illustrative wording, not a live screenshot of her — she is fictional):

- What can I cook in 15 minutes with a tin of tomatoes?
- Do I need a recipe?
- How do I stop burning onions?
- What if I only have one pan?
- Can I cook after 8pm without a “proper shop”?

Her first video in chapter 0 already used the “you don’t need fourteen ingredients” branch. The onion-burning branch is video two. She does *not* make a video about “best air fryers of 2026” just because it appears in autocomplete; it is not her life and not her pillar.

The [idea tree worksheet](templates/idea-tree.md) has this example and a blank tree.

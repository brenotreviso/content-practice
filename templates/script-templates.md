## 1. Curiosity gap (information gap)

**Theory:** Loewenstein (1994). See the chapter.

> You probably think ________.
> I thought that too, until ________.
> Here is what actually happens: ________.
> So, if you try this, do ________.

**Maya:** “You probably think a Tuesday dinner needs a long recipe. I thought that too, until I started cooking from one tin and one pan. Here is what actually happens…”

## 3. Expectation violation

**Theory:** Burgoon’s Expectancy Violations Theory (1978, later work). A cheap or mean surprise is a negative violation.

> You would expect ________.
> Instead, ________.
> That matters because ________.

**Maya:** “You would expect a nicer meal to need more ingredients. Instead, the thing that improved mine was cooking the onions longer, not adding more food.”

## 5. Promise, break, frustrate, solve

**Unverified as a formal published title.** A structure taught in this guide. Map it onto curiosity, expectation violation, delayed payoff, and PAS.

> Promise: by the end you will know ________.
> Expected path: most people would ________.
> That’s where it usually goes wrong: ________.
> The fix: ________.
> (Optional CTA): if you try it, tell me ________.

**Maya:** Promise: you will be able to cook the tomato pan without the bitter onions. Expected path: rush them on a high heat. Where it goes wrong: they burn and the whole pan tastes of that. Fix: lower heat, more time, a pinch of salt early.

## 6. But / therefore (not “and then”)

**Source:** Parker & Stone, NYU writing class (2011). Rewrite every “and then”.

> I did ________, **but** ________, **therefore** ________.

**Maya:** “I bought more ingredients, *but* the dinner still tasted flat, *therefore* the problem was the onions, not the shopping list.”

## 10. Dan Harmon’s Story Circle

You / Need / Go / Search / Find / Take / Return / Change — one short clause each.

**Maya (a walk):** You: she comes home tired. Need: she wants to leave the flat without a “proper plan”. Go: the canal towpath. Search: she almost turns back at the first bridge. Find: a twenty-minute loop. Take: she is late for the last shop. Return: the same kitchen. Change: the walk is now the start of dinner, not a thing she failed to do.
